# Narration — QNX Real-Time Patient Monitor (target ~75 s)
#
# Read at a calm, steady pace. The video shows the live serial console
# of QNX 8.0 running in QEMU: the monitor's dashboard, the CPU-load
# phase, the tachycardia alarm, and the final deadline summary.

This is a real-time patient monitor running on QNX 8.0, inside QEMU.

Six threads, all fixed-priority. The alarm thread runs every 100
milliseconds at the highest priority. ECG sampling at 100 hertz,
oxygen saturation at 50 hertz, blood pressure every 15 seconds.

Watch what happens at 20 seconds: a low-priority task saturates the
CPU. The dashboard keeps updating once a second, and the jitter numbers
stay in the low milliseconds.

At 45 seconds, the patient goes into tachycardia — heart rate jumps to
132. The alarm fires in under a second, while the CPU is still loaded.

At 60 seconds the load stops and vitals recover.

Here is the proof, measured on the target, not asserted: the
safety-critical alarm thread missed zero deadlines in 900 activations,
while the CPU was saturated. That is what a hard real-time operating
system is for — in a patient monitor, the alarm path must fire on time,
no matter what else the system is doing.
